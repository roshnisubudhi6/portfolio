The context

Jio was building commerce (JioMart) and needed its own payments layer rather than depending on a third party. Its businesses processed a large volume of digital payments (wallet, cards, UPI, net banking) through an external orchestrator. Every successful transaction paid that vendor a fee, and Jio's routing, offers and roadmap depended on someone else's priorities. I took JioOnePay from 0 to 1 as its founding PM.

The problem

Jio was paying per-transaction fees and depending on an external orchestrator for a capability at the core of its commerce and recharge businesses.

  • Cost: fees scaled with every rupee processed. At early volumes this was already around ₹1 Cr a year, and it grew with volume.
  • Control: Jio didn't own routing or retry logic, which drive success rates.
  • Flexibility: offers and payment-flow changes sat in a vendor's queue.

At Jio's scale, renting payments infrastructure was a recurring cost and a dependency. Owning it was a strategic advantage.

My role

Founding PM: owned strategy, roadmap and launch from 0 to 1. I worked with engineering, risk and compliance, partnerships and the JioMart business team, and was the payments subject-matter owner for the product, including regulatory constraints.

JioOnePay was a founding team inside Reliance/Jio, with the backing of a large organization and the ambiguity of a new product.

Key decisions

1 · The orchestration core

We built our own orchestration layer first, because it directly answered the cost and control problem: Jio would stop paying per-transaction fees on its own traffic and would own routing.

2 · B2B Promotional Engine

Offers were the capability that had kept merchants on the incumbent. I owned the 0→1 build: card discounts, cashback, and eligibility rules such as one card, one offer.

The hard part was an RBI tokenization rule. We couldn't store card details, but eligibility needed to know whether a card had already used an offer. I called other payment providers to see how they handled the same constraint, then worked with engineering to land on one-way hashing: mapping hashed card numbers to customer accounts so we could check prior usage without storing raw card data.

We launched the MVP with JioMart's first annual sale as the first merchant. It drove a 13× increase in electronics GMV, and the sale repositioned JioMart as an electronics destination, not just a grocery app. The merchandising team could now create, activate and pause offers from a dashboard instead of waiting on engineering, and the platform held 99.9% uptime through peak sales.

3 · Self-serve onboarding

A merchant dashboard cut time-to-value from a week to an hour, and that is what let JioOnePay scale beyond Jio's own properties.

We built it for Jio's businesses first. It became sophisticated enough to take to market, and the 30+ external merchants we acquired turned a cost center into a new revenue line for Jio Platforms.

What I learned

  • When you hit a regulatory or technical wall, the answer is usually already being solved somewhere in the market. The PM's job is to know who to call, extract the signal and translate it back to your context. That's how we got past tokenization.
  • Platforms get adopted on whether the next customer says yes. Build for the merchant you haven't won yet, not only the ones you have.
